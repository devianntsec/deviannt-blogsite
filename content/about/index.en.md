<h2> Annais Molina Fuentes | devianntsec </h2>

I take software apart until I find the exact point where it stops behaving
the way it was designed to. A crash is the start of the question, never the
answer. What follows is static analysis of compiled code, dynamic
instrumentation, memory under a microscope, and tracing execution paths
nobody planned for anyone to see. It always ends the same way: the full
root cause and a proof of concept that actually runs, not a tidy hypothesis
sitting in a PDF.

---

<h2> Focus areas </h2>

- **Reverse engineering** — static and dynamic analysis, binary instrumentation, decompilation workflows
- **Vulnerability research** — CVE analysis, root cause identification, working exploit development
- **Web & application security** — server-side logic flaws, deserialization, prototype pollution, auth bypass
- **Technical documentation** — full exploit chains with architecture diagrams, sequence flows, and steps that are actually reproducible, not just "should work in theory"

---

<h2> This space </h2>

This is where I publish the process, not the headline. Every post
reconstructs the full path: the dead ends, the hypothesis that finally
worked, and everything that came after the initial finding — which is
usually longer and more interesting than the finding itself.

CVE advisories and PoCs live on [GitHub](https://github.com/devianntsec).

<h2> Background </h2>

Cybersecurity degree from UCAM and the Campus Internacional de
